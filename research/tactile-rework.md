# Tactile rework after player rejection

The owner played the previous pass and rejected its asset feel, pulling, release and merges. Passing continuity and solver tests did not establish appealing animation. The existing phone contact sheets show only a small silhouette difference between poses and a merge that reads as two figures shrinking out.

Code inspection found a concrete defect: directional deformation used `dx² − dy²`, which becomes zero for diagonal input. Replace that approximation with an oriented volume-preserving transform and verify axis and diagonal pulls. Keep face and limbs attached to the same rig.

Jonathan Cooper's [game animation principles](https://www.gameanim.com/2019/05/15/the-12-principles-of-animation-in-video-games/) discuss related-direction squash/stretch, volume preservation, blocking clear gameplay poses, overlapping body parts and returning control before follow-through ends. Bunbreak's design inference is to make the held input a visible loaded pose, use immediate physics with a readable launch silhouette, and make a merge a compressed contact/reveal/landing performance. Exact timings and amplitudes are our own tuning hypotheses. The GDC Vault candidate reference redirected to maintenance; its talk was not reviewed or used as evidence.

Implementation sequence: (1) oriented dough deformation with distinct loading/release/impact and safe cancellation; (2) responsive expressions with tier-specific appeal and Pip's recognizable silhouette; (3) an elastic drag cue tied to the real pointer; (4) a merge with visible contact, a compressed resulting bun, reveal, hop and landing. Use local effects to support the face, not cover it. Camera remains 45° by default and the palette study remains deferred.

The owner's answers refine (1) to a localized anchored teardrop, with the finger-side dough following the input and the opposite side staying near the start. The face stays on the fuller region, eyes follow the finger, and release snaps into the shot with two soft jiggles. This needs deformation within the dough silhouette rather than only a rotated scale of the whole character. Body volume and face attachment must be reviewed honestly: a unit determinant for a rig transform alone does not prove volume preservation of a nonlinear vertex deformation.

Review at native phone size and in close-up crops: weak/strong loading, horizontal/vertical/diagonal input, reversal, cancellation, launch, both merges and winning. Compare before and after browser frames; inspect the motion, not merely the number of generated files. Verify immediate launch, unchanged solved routes, stable positive transforms, reduced motion, repeated merges, pause/reset and 30/60/120 schedules. A stronger visible performance is the goal; final subjective quality and phone hardware behavior require further playtesting.
